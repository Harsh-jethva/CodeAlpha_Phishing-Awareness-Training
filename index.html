Recognizing phishing emails and fake websites

Phishing tries to steal credentials, money, or access by pretending to be someone you trust.

  • Sender addresses may mimic a brand (for example support@paypa1-security.com).
  • Links often hide the real destination — hover or long-press before clicking.
  • Urgent threats (“account suspended today”) pressure you to act without thinking.
  • Requests for passwords, MFA codes, or remote access are almost never legitimate.
  • Fake sites may use typosquatting (faceboook.com), HTTP-only login pages, or odd fonts.

Social engineering tactics

Attackers manipulate people, not just computers. Knowing their playbook helps you resist it.

  • Pretexting: inventing a scenario (IT audit, vendor callback).
  • Authority: impersonating executives or government to demand compliance.
  • Urgency and fear: artificial deadlines to bypass verification.
  • Familiarity: referencing coworkers or projects from public sources.
  • Helpfulness: offering “support” to harvest credentials.

Best practices

Simple habits dramatically reduce risk — use them every day.

  • Verify out-of-band: call a known number from the company website, not from the email.
  • Use bookmarked URLs for banking and email; avoid links in unexpected messages.
  • Enable MFA on important accounts; prefer app-based or hardware keys.
  • Report suspicious mail via your security team or your provider’s report-phishing option.
  • Keep software updated; use a password manager for unique strong passwords.

Real-world examples

These patterns show up across industries — the details change, the tricks don’t.

CEO fraud

An urgent email from “the CEO” asks you to buy gift cards for a client and send the codes by reply. The reply-to domain is slightly wrong.

Tax / refund lure

A message claims you’re owed a refund and must “confirm” bank details on a site that looks like your government portal — but the URL is unrelated.

Shared-file trap

You get a “shared document” notification from a service you use; the button goes to a login page that steals your password.
